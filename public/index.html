What desk research settled

Four things that were open are now answered from published vendor documentation, and none of them needed an email.

  • Stay Ai on Tapcart is Enterprise‑only. Stay Ai's own help centre states the integration is available to Tapcart Enterprise customers only, which settles the tier half of question one. The success fee is still unanswered.
  • The Stay Ai portal is a webview, not native UI. It requires a Web Bridge SDK and loads the existing portal inside the app without redirecting to a browser. That is an embedded web view by construction, so “embedded natively” meant embedded, not native.
  • A Tapcart–Rivo integration exists, in beta, via custom blocks. Rivo's product page describes showing loyalty data and redeeming rewards through custom blocks, with the refer‑a‑friend page as a custom screen. Their help centre calls the integration beta, needing a Rivo CSM to enable it and coordination across Rivo, Tapcart and a development team. Reads cover points balance, credits and transaction history; writes cover store‑credit redemption with automatic discount code generation.
  • Appbrew publishes its pricing. Their Shopify App Store listing lists Starter at $199, Pro at $599 and Enterprise at $1,099 a month, with native subscription management appearing only at Enterprise. Klaviyo and ReCharge are listed under Works with. Stay Ai is not.

Two cautions. Rivo's product page says custom blocks while its help centre article doesn't mention them — worth confirming which is current, since it decides whether loyalty is configuration or build. And third‑party blogs quoted Appbrew's Pro tier at $499 against the $599 on the App Store listing, which is the same unreliability that got pricing pulled from this document in the first place. The figures above are from the vendors' own pages.

The pattern under all three requirements

Read together rather than row by row, the three verdicts say the same thing. Tapcart's capability is real and it is Tapcart's. Flows run through Klaviyo and Attentive, but composition — campaigns, segments, in‑app messages, A/B tests — happens in Tapcart's own dashboard, against its own Segment Builder.

That is not a failure, it's a different operating model: Klaviyo becomes the data layer and Tapcart becomes the messaging surface, instead of Klaviyo owning both. It is worth deciding whether that is acceptable before negotiating price, because no amount of custom‑block spend changes it. Custom blocks alter what a screen renders, not who owns the send.

Appbrew still carries two blanks for the opposite reason to before: its pricing is now public, but nothing published shows Stay Ai support at all. Its total is scored out of five rows, not seven, and isn't directly comparable until that lands.

The phasing argument no longer holds. MVP starts on the customised path.

This note used to say that nothing in MVP needed a custom block, and to watch the counter on the board for the moment that stopped being true. That moment has arrived. Getting segments, behavioural events and customer identity out of Tapcart and into Klaviyo, Attentive and Rivo is custom work, and all of it sits in MVP — it is the lifecycle plumbing the entire churn case runs on, not a personalisation nicety that can wait.

So the two Tapcart columns were never phase one and phase two for this scope. The standard build does not reach MVP. Heavily customised is the entry point, not the upgrade.

That moves the only things the two columns were separated by. Build spend and time to launch stop being standard‑build numbers, and the gap to bespoke narrows on customisation ceiling — the axis where bespoke already led. Whether it narrows far enough to reopen bespoke depends on the custom‑block quote, which is now the most valuable number you don't have.

What survives intact: define the held‑out control cohort in Stay Ai before launch, and judge whatever gets built on subscriber churn against it. That was never contingent on which path you take.

Ten things still to get in writing

Everything in the matrix assumes Tapcart Enterprise. Desk research closed four of the previous ten from published vendor documentation — the Stay Ai tier, the portal being a webview, the existence of a Rivo integration, and Appbrew's pricing. What follows is what documentation cannot answer, plus one new question the research created.

  1. Does a success fee apply to Stay Ai at Enterprise?The tier is settled — Stay Ai's help centre says Tapcart Enterprise only. The fee structure was never stated and their pricing section came through empty. This is now a one‑line answer.
  2. Which subscription actions does the embedded portal actually support?New, and it matters. Stay Ai's Tapcart article lists skipping, swapping, discount codes, add‑ons and cancellation with a save flow — and does not mention pause, frequency change, or payment and address updates. Tapcart's own page does claim frequency. Pause, frequency and payment/address are all MVP features on the board, so if the portal omits them, three MVP rows are wrong.
  3. Campaigns: can they be authored in Klaviyo, or only in Tapcart?Flows are settled. Campaigns are not, and nothing published resolves it. If they can only be written in Tapcart, lifecycle authors push in two systems permanently. Ask specifically whether that is configuration or a custom block.
  4. The exact list of events and properties that reach Klaviyo, and whether they're segmentable and triggerable.Named events landing as metrics, in real time. No published source lists them. Ask for the schema, not a logo.
  5. Rivo: is the beta generally available, does it expose tier status, and who builds and owns the custom blocks?The integration exists and is delivered through custom blocks, so loyalty in MVP is build rather than configuration. Their help centre calls it beta and requires coordination across three parties. Confirm it is production‑ready, that tier status and not just points balance is available, and whose budget the blocks come from.
  6. Can Klaviyo drive in‑app messages, or only Tapcart's Inbox?Deploying without an app release is settled — App Studio does that. What isn't settled is whether lifecycle can target and trigger those messages from Klaviyo, or whether they must be built in Tapcart's Segment Builder. Same shape as the campaign question, and the answer is probably the same.
  7. Who maintains custom blocks through platform updates, and does Enterprise support cover them?Now the most expensive unknown on the list. MVP needs custom blocks for identity, events, opt‑in, loyalty and rewards. This decides whether that is an asset or a liability we own forever.
  8. Does “managed Apple & Google developer portal” mean they administer accounts we own, or hold them?The lock‑in question. We need the accounts, the listing and the push tokens.
  9. Appbrew: does Stay Ai work at all, and a live demo on our own catalogue.Pricing is answered — $199, $599 and $1,099 a month, published. The harder gap is that Stay Ai appears nowhere in their listing while ReCharge does. If there is no Stay Ai support, Appbrew is not a candidate regardless of price.
  10. Two reference customers on Stay Ai at similar revenue, on the phone.Kaged and Credo are Tapcart's chosen references. Ask specifically for a Stay Ai brand, and ask them where they author campaigns.

One thing to settle internally

Success metric is subscriber churn in Stay Ai cohort data, not app revenue in Branch. Branch attributes deep‑linked conversions but not organic app opens, so it structurally undercounts app‑driven revenue. Churn on a held‑out cohort is the only clean read, and it needs defining before launch.

And one about ourselves

Bespoke scores worst on time to launch and dev burden for a reason: G Fuel migrated off a custom app over a developer bottleneck, and Your Super refused a 6–8 month build. Whether that applies to us depends on whether we have in‑house mobile capacity or would be signing an agency retainer.

Business case behind this. 95,753 active subscriptions losing roughly 14% a month; 91% of Shopify revenue subscription‑linked; 93% of sessions on mobile. A sustained one‑point churn improvement is worth about $3.1M in annual run‑rate revenue. Via Omni — topics stay_ai, performance, ga4_sessions_wip, July 2026.
